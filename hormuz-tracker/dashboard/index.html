Read this first. The measured series counts AIS-visible transits in five vessel classes from IMF PortWatch. It excludes gas carriers entirely (LNG/LPG are not in the source), plus passenger and small/local craft, and it misses vessels transiting with AIS off — common since the strait closure of 28 Feb 2026. PortWatch's 2025 level (~86/day) is therefore not comparable to all-vessel headline counts (~125–138/day); use it for trend and change within the series. PortWatch publishes weekly, roughly 7–10 days behind.

Modelled monthly crude / products split — ESTIMATE, not measured

PortWatch has a single tanker bucket; no free source separates crude from products. The split below applies a modelled crude count share of 45% (band 34–59%, ±~12pp) to the measured monthly tanker total: EIA's crude volume share (~70.5%) converted with average parcel sizes (crude 1.3M bbl, products 0.45M bbl — a VLCC carries ~4× an MR tanker, so crude's count share is far below its volume share). Monthly only, never daily.
Mar–Sep 2026 warning: in the crisis months the share is applied to very small AIS-visible tanker counts (29–162/month, vs ~1,200 pre-crisis) while most traffic transited with AIS off. The split for those months is an arithmetic decomposition of the visible residue — it is not a measurement of actual crude/products flows and cannot be verified from free data.

LNG carriers — ESTIMATE from the load side (absent from PortWatch)

Modelled from EIA's H1 2025 Hormuz LNG flow (11.4 Bcf/d) ÷ 4.2 Bcf average cargo ≈ 2.7 loaded exits/day (±25%), applied only to pre-crisis months. Crisis months have no reliable free anchor and are left blank, except one sourced point: Oct 2026, 31 loaded exits (S&P Global via press). Total transits ≈ 2× loaded exits (ballast entries).
Forward note: since 2026-11-15 the live collector identifies LNG carriers per-ship against a curated IMO fleet registry (301 hulls) — a measured series. As it accumulates, its transit rates will calibrate this historical estimate; the estimate itself is not relabelled.

Methods and limits

  • Measured data: IMF PortWatch daily chokepoint counts (satellite AIS via Kpler / UN Global Platform), pulled in full (2019–present) from its public ArcGIS service. Classes: tanker, container, dry bulk, general cargo, ro-ro. There is no direction field and no crude/products/gas split in the source; tanker deadweight-capacity fields read 0 on some crisis days with tankers present and are not charted for that reason.
  • Modelled split: see the estimate box above. Error band ±10–15pp on the share, wider in dark-transit months. The split assumes the ballast (inbound) fleet mix mirrors the loaded (outbound) mix.
  • LNG: ±20–30% in normal times; crisis months are gaps, not zeros.
  • Forward collector: since 15 Nov 2026, a local collector records ship-level AIS transits (two-zone crossing detection at 56.65°E, direction from track sequence, broad AIS ship-type classes) from Open Waters AIS, which aggregates AISStream and AISHub. AIS type codes do not separate crude/products/LNG; since 15 Nov 2026 LNG carriers are instead identified per-ship against a curated IMO-keyed fleet registry (301 hulls; matched crossings are measured and named — see FEED.md in the repo), while crude vs products remains inseparable per-ship. Direct AISStream collection is blocked by its in-message-body API key, which the secure-credential layer cannot place; details in the project DESIGN.md.
  • Cross-checks used in design: EIA World Oil Transit Chokepoints (volumes), UNCTAD/Clarksons snapshot shares, UKMTO/JMIC weekly advisories (direction/subtype, crisis period only).

Sources: Kpler; UN Global Platform; IMF PortWatch (portwatch.imf.org). Model anchors: EIA World Oil Transit Chokepoints; S&P Global via press (Oct 2026 LNG). Personal research project — free/public data only. Data as of .
